Profile

About

I am an AI Research Assistant at the P&G Digital Accelerator at the University of Cincinnati and a PhD student in Computer Science.

My current research investigates failure modes and trustworthy decision-making in multi-agent LLM systems, including consensus collapse in retrieval-augmented generation, evidence-sensitive agent re-invocation, and causal auditing of apparent collusion.

I design methods that test whether agent agreement is independently supported, identify which agents should be re-executed when evidence changes, and separate coordinated behavior from shared calibration effects. My earlier work focused on federated learning for privacy-preserving agriculture, computer vision, and applied data systems.

Research

Questions, methods, and application domains shaping my work.

Multi-agent RAG

Consensus collapse and evidence independence

Studying when apparent agreement among agents reflects shared or insufficient evidence rather than independent support.

Evidence sensitivity

Selective agent re-invocation

Calibrating evidence-change scores to re-execute only the agents whose outputs are materially affected.

Causal auditing

Collusion versus correlated calibration

Using causal-mediation analysis to distinguish coordinated behavior from correlations induced by shared calibration.
